## Purpose

A pre-mortem asks the team to imagine that the plan has already failed, then
explain why. Teams find it hard to criticize a plan they are about to approve.
Assuming failure removes that social pressure and turns doubt into a task.

## Steps

1. Brief the team on the plan in its final form.
2. State the premise: "It is one year from now. The plan failed badly."
3. Give each person five minutes to write every reason for the failure, alone
   and in silence.
4. Go around the room. Each person reads one reason per turn until all are listed.
5. Group the reasons. Rate each group on likelihood and impact.
6. For the top three groups, name an owner and one change to the plan, an early
   warning sign, or a decision to stop.
7. Revisit the list at the first major milestone.

## Common failure modes

- The most senior person speaks first, and everyone else edits their list.
- Reasons stay vague ("poor execution") instead of specific ("the second
  contractor cannot start until the permit clears").
- The session produces a list but no changes to the plan.
- The team treats the exercise as a formality after the decision is already made.
