## Purpose

A business case should answer five separate questions. Proposals often argue
one question well, usually the economic one, and leave the others thin. This
lite version keeps the five-part structure but fits on a few pages, so a small
team can use it early, before a full appraisal.

## Steps

1. **Strategic case.** Why act at all? State the problem, the objectives, and how
   the proposal fits wider goals. Name what happens if nothing is done.
2. **Economic case.** Which option gives the best value? Compare at least three
   options, including doing the minimum. Show costs, benefits, and the main risks
   for each.
3. **Commercial case.** Can the preferred option be bought or built? Describe the
   market, the procurement route, and how risk is shared with contractors.
4. **Financial case.** Is it affordable? Show the funding sources, the cash-flow
   profile, and the effect on budgets in each year.
5. **Management case.** Can it be delivered? Name the owner, the governance, the
   milestones, and how benefits will be measured after completion.
6. Write a one-page summary that answers each question in two or three sentences.

## Common failure modes

- Choosing the preferred option first and building the economic case around it.
- Counting benefits that would happen anyway.
- An affordable plan with no realistic route to market, or the reverse.
- A management case with no named owner and no post-completion review.
